Announced by his family

A Brief Journey With Alzheimer's and Lewy Body Dementia

“We announce with great sorrow the passing of Sir Patrick Stewart yesterday at his home in Los Angeles, in the loving care of his beloved wife Sunny, following a brief and poignant journey with Alzheimer's Disease and Lewy Body Dementia,” the family statement read. His spokesperson, Kelly Bush Novak, confirmed the death.

Mirfield · Yorkshire

From a Yorkshire Childhood to the Royal Shakespeare Company

Born on 13 July 1940 in Mirfield, in the West Riding of Yorkshire, Stewart joined the Royal Shakespeare Company in 1966 and stayed until 1982. He won two Laurence Olivier Awards and a Grammy Award, and was knighted by Queen Elizabeth II in 2010 for services to drama.

Star Trek · 1987–1994

Captain Jean-Luc Picard: Seven Seasons on the Enterprise

Stewart gained international stardom as Captain Jean-Luc Picard in Star Trek: The Next Generation (1987–1994), which ran for seven seasons and 178 episodes. He later said the role changed everything for him — and that his years with the Royal Shakespeare Company were, in his words, preparation for sitting in the captain's chair.

Four films, then a return

Four Star Trek Films — and Picard, 2020 to 2023

After the series ended, Stewart reprised Picard in four Star Trek films from 1994 to 2002, beginning with Star Trek Generations. He returned to the role once more in Star Trek: Picard, which ran for three seasons from 2020 to 2023 and reunited him with his Next Generation castmates.

X-Men · 2000–2017

Professor Charles Xavier, From X-Men to Logan

On film, Stewart gained further stardom as Professor Charles Xavier in the X-Men series, beginning with X-Men in 2000 and including X2, X-Men: The Last Stand, X-Men: Days of Future Past and Logan in 2017. He returned to the role in Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness in 2022.

“Captain Picard Stood for the Best of Us”

Captain Picard stood for the best of us, and so did the man who played him.” Marvel Studios called him “a singular talent, a generous collaborator, and a true gentleman.”

His final screen role

Avengers: Doomsday, and a Family Request

Stewart is set to appear posthumously as Professor Xavier in Avengers: Doomsday, which will be his final screen appearance. In lieu of flowers, his family asked for memorial contributions to the Johns Hopkins Alzheimer's Disease Research Center Fund and Alzheimer's Foundation of America Caregiver Resources.

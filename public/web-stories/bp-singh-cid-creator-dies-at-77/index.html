B. P. Singh · 1949–2026

From Dehradun to Doordarshan

Born in Dehradun, Singh trained in cinematography at the Film and Television Institute of India (FTII) in Pune. In 1973 he joined Doordarshan as a news cameraman, a job he held for a decade, before making telefilms including Sirf Chaar Din (1985) and Ek Shunya Shunya (1988).

B. P. Singh · 1949–2026

CID: The Show That Never Left

When Sony TV entered India in 1995, Singh created CID. It began airing in 1998 and ran to 1,651 episodes, becoming one of India's longest-running series. Singh himself appeared on screen as DCP Shamsher Singh Chitrole.

B. P. Singh · 1949–2026

The 111-Minute Single Shot

In October 2004 Singh directed The Inheritance, a CID episode filmed in a single continuous take of 111 minutes. It entered the Limca Book of Records and the Guinness World Records for the longest continuous television shot.

Aahat — and Everything After

Beyond CID, Singh made the horror series Aahat, which ran from 1995 to 2015, and Gutur Gu, considered India's first silent television show. His later work included SuperCops Vs Super Villains and the thriller series Abhay.

B. P. Singh · 1949–2026

Teacher, Chairman, Friend

Singh served as chairman of FTII's governing council from 2018 to 2020 and as president of the Indian Film & Television Directors' Association. Actor Karan Oberoi, who began his career with Singh, called him “a hard taskmaster but also a gentle soul” who paid artists from his own pocket when a project lost money.

B. P. Singh · 1949–2026

“He Had No Other Life”

Dayanand Shetty, who played Daya in CID, remembered Singh's devotion: “BP Singh ji had no other life, other than making content. He was dedicated to his work and was very passionate about it.” Pandit put it simply: his work “will continue to be an inspiration to generations to come.”

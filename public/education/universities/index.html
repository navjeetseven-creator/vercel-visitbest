What this hub covers

This hub covers universities and university finance programmes through three guides, each built from one named public dataset rather than a VisitBest judgement of overall quality. Two guides use a single research indicator from the CWTS Leiden Ranking Open Edition 2025: one orders 100 universities worldwide by it, and the other applies the same measure through the ranking’s Europe filter, which includes the United Kingdom and Switzerland and is broader than the European Union. The third guide sets institution-wide research aside and orders 100 large United States bachelor’s programmes in Finance, General by their reported completions in NCES IPEDS data. The hub does not cover every university or every programme, and none of the three tables measures teaching quality.

Metric ordering or ranking? Read the label

All three tables are ordered, but each order means something narrower than a “best universities” ranking, and each guide states its own limits. The world and Europe tables use one research-volume indicator from the CWTS Leiden Ranking Open Edition 2025: institutions are ordered by P(top 10%), the fractional volume of their publications falling among the most cited 10% in their field and year, counted over 2020–2023 publications in all sciences, core publications only, with a minimum of 1,000 publications. That is a count of highly cited research output, not a score for teaching, student experience, affordability or graduate employment, and the guides are explicit that it is not an overall ranking: a larger institution can lead on volume while a smaller one records a higher highly cited share, PP(top 10%). The edition matters as much as the indicator — the 2025 edition describes 2020–2023 research, so a guide checked in 2026 is not reporting 2026 results, and a different edition, field selection or ordering indicator would produce a different list. The finance table is not a quality ranking either: it orders qualifying US institutions by reported bachelor’s completions in Finance, General (CIP 52.0801) in NCES IPEDS 2024 data for the year from 1 July 2023 to 30 June 2024. Completions are historical awards, not current enrolment or places available now, and a finance programme reported under a broader business code sits outside the extract however strong it may be. In all three tables, read the number beside a name as the stated measure only: a position here does not confirm that an applicant is eligible for a course, that it is affordable, or that an outcome such as a job or a salary will follow.

Before you apply: five checks

  1. Start with the exact award and subject, then check that department’s current entry requirements: institution-wide research strength can hide real differences between departments, and a strong research position does not establish eligibility for any particular course. Use the official course catalogue for compulsory modules, assessment and prerequisites; research applicants should read a prospective supervisor’s recent work and confirm that supervision is available, and taught-course applicants should compare practical facilities, contact hours and academic support.
  2. Verify the teaching language and the test evidence: an English course title does not establish that every module, placement or clinical component is taught or assessed in English, and language scores, standardised tests, translations and references all need preparation time. Record the award, its duration, the required credits and any thesis or placement, because degree structures differ between countries and between institutions.
  3. Follow the actual application route, using official sources only for dates: some programmes use a national portal and others take direct or departmental applications, and only the current official course page states the route and the deadline for your applicant category and intake. After an offer, read the acceptance, deposit and refund terms before paying anything, and check whether accommodation is guaranteed, open to application or arranged externally — those are different offers.
  4. Calculate the complete study cost before comparing places: the current fee schedule for your applicant category and academic year, plus housing, meals, health cover, equipment and travel. Keep the original currency visible in the comparison, and separate a confirmed award from a scholarship you hope to receive — competitive awards are not automatic reductions, and renewal can depend on progress or a fresh application, so the budget has to work without an unconfirmed award. For the US finance programmes, compare the net cost for your category — in-state, out-of-state or international — using aid actually available to you, and confirm whether entry to the finance major is direct or follows an initial business curriculum with its own conditions.
  5. Check recognition and outcome evidence separately from these tables: a professional qualification needs recognition in the country where you plan to work, and that is decided by the receiving institution or the relevant regulator — no ranking position can guarantee it. Where a programme reports employment or salary outcomes, compare only figures with the same graduating cohort, response rate and definitions, because these tables supply no placement, internship or salary evidence; international students should take work-authorisation guidance from official sources only.

How to use the comparisons

  1. Read the source, edition and geographic limits.
  2. Shortlist feasible programmes or year groups.
  3. Save current admission, qualification and fee evidence.
  4. Resolve missing answers with the actual campus.

Top 100 High Schools in England: State Secondary-School Guide

100 open, state-funded, non-selective secondary schools across England’s nine regions. Directory selection, not a results league table.

The term high school is commonly used in searches, while England’s official directory describes these institutions as secondary schools. This guide selects 100 open, non-selective state-funded secondary-school records across England’s nine regions. It covers academies and local-authority-maintained schools. The list is a way to research locations and official records, not a ranking of examination performance. A family’s next step is the school’s current admission arrangement and the relevant local authority’s application guidance.

Check admission and catchment details

Non-selective does not mean unrestricted admission. Oversubscription criteria, residence, distance and other published priorities can affect allocation. Read the arrangement for the intended entry year. Some schools have separate rules for specialist places or sixth-form entry. The directory establishes school identity and status; it does not establish that a particular address qualifies for a place.

Compare curriculum and progression

Ask about compulsory subjects, option blocks and availability in the intended year. A school may provide secondary education without an on-site sixth form. Older pupils need a feasible route to A levels, vocational study or another next stage. Check prerequisites and the actual subject combination rather than a general statement about broad choice. A school’s official course information should support the plan.

Read inspection and performance together

Inspection reports and performance measures describe different aspects of a school. Use the correct year, measure and cohort when comparing outcomes. Differences in pupil intake affect interpretation. Avoid treating a single headline as a complete judgement about the child’s likely experience. The guide’s numbers are row identifiers; no examination score is used to order them.

Ask about everyday support

A family can ask how the school supports a pupil who falls behind, experiences bullying or needs language or learning help. Confirm the reporting route and how parents receive progress updates. Visit at a normal time if possible and examine travel, homework and extracurricular routines. A written support plan is more useful than an unqualified promise that every pupil thrives.

Prepare an affordable, realistic application

Record the relevant application deadline and evidence required by the admission authority. Test the daily journey and compare transport, uniforms, devices and activities. Include feasible preferences rather than assuming proximity alone guarantees allocation. Ask about transition support from primary school. The final shortlist should combine eligibility, provision and a manageable daily routine.

Decision checklist

  • Check the admission year
  • Read allocation priorities
  • Compare option subjects
  • Plan the post-16 route
  • Test the daily journey

Shortlist worksheet

For three feasible options, record the exact programme or year group, current eligibility, full cost, support provision, travel and the official evidence date. Leave a missing answer unresolved instead of estimating it.

Questions before you decide

No. The filter selects non-selective state-funded secondary schools.

Does the list cover the whole UK?

No. Its official data scope is England.

Is high school a separate official category here?

No. This guide uses the official Secondary phase.

Does every school have a sixth form?

No. Check the directory and current school provision.

Are these the top 100 by results?

No. It is a regional directory sample with no results ranking.

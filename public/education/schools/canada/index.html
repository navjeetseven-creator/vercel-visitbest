Schools in Canada

What this hub covers

This hub covers schools in Canada through two guides, each a 100-entry directory sample rather than a results table. The private-schools guide lists 97 schools from the CAIS directory, spanning several provinces, plus three Ontario private-school records from Ontario’s private-school contact list, added by a location and alphabetical round-robin; its grade ranges vary, so not every entry is a secondary school. The high-schools guide lists 60 CAIS-directory records with secondary grades, reaching several provinces, plus 40 Ontario public secondary schools. Public-school coverage is Ontario only, and neither guide is every school in Canada.

Directory sample or ranking? Read the label

The “Top 100” in each guide’s title counts the entries in the sample — neither table is a rank order, and both guides state this themselves. The private-schools guide is not a government-endorsed national ranking; the high-schools guide states that its rows carry no performance rank or judgement. In both tables the list numbers are navigation identifiers, placed so the sample spreads geographically; no examination score ordered them. A CAIS entry records membership, and an Ontario contact-list entry records a listing — neither endorses quality, and a listing does not establish authority to grant diploma credits. Only a published metric, with a named source and edition, can support a ranking.

Before you apply: five checks

  1. Start with the province, the board and the qualification: education in Canada is organised by province, and completion credits differ between provinces. For a public school, identify the school board — in these guides the public records are Ontario boards only — and use the province’s current diploma requirements and the school’s course list. For an Ontario private school, check its diploma-credit record: the contact list states whether a school reports offering OSSD credits, a listing is not an endorsement, and CAIS membership is a separate record.
  2. Check eligibility and residency honestly: a directory entry does not establish that a particular child can take a place. Ontario public-school access can depend on address, board policy, programme and applicant status, and a specialist programme may use a separate process; private schools set their own admission procedures. A school admitting international pupils may still impose residence or custodianship conditions, and international tuition or residence requirements need a direct check before any non-refundable payment.
  3. Confirm the grade range, grade placement and language of instruction: check the grade range first, because not every private-guide entry is secondary. Ask how the school places pupils arriving from another province or country and how prior learning is assessed, since grade numbering and completion timing can differ from their previous system. Confirm the intended grade’s language of instruction and what language provision is offered — the guides treat language support as a conversation about the child, including who provides it and any separate charge.
  4. Take documents, dates and costs only from current official sources: these records are checked snapshots publishing no application dates or fee schedules, and neither guide publishes fee ranges. For a private school, tuition is only part of the bill — compare registration, uniforms, devices, transport, trips, meals and activities, ask separately for boarding costs, and treat financial assistance as confirmed only with the award and renewal conditions in writing. For a public school, compare transport, meals, devices and activity charges even where no tuition is charged; leave unpublished figures unresolved.
  5. Test the daily journey, the winter term and the support on offer: compare the real journey at both ends of the day, remembering it is a winter journey for much of the year — distance, transport and weather disruption decide whether the school is workable daily. Ask how teachers communicate progress, how learning support and guidance counselling are arranged, and what happens if arrival is delayed or a course changes. Read the latest inspection or school review, where one is published, alongside the school’s response, and confirm the route to the next stage, including transcript transfer.

For schools outside Canada, start from the Top Schools Worldwide hub, which links the same labelled guides by region.

How to use the comparisons

  1. Read the source, edition and geographic limits.
  2. Shortlist feasible programmes or year groups.
  3. Save current admission, qualification and fee evidence.
  4. Resolve missing answers with the actual campus.

A directory listing, membership record and academic performance measure answer different questions. They are labelled separately throughout these guides.

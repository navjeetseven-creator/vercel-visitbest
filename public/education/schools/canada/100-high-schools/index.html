Top 100 High Schools in Canada: A Sourced Secondary-School Guide

60 Canadian CAIS secondary-school records and 40 Ontario public secondary schools. Public-school coverage is Ontario only.

Canada does not have one admission system or one national high-school league table. This guide combines 60 CAIS-directory schools with secondary grade provision and 40 Ontario public secondary-school records. The private-school component reaches several provinces; the public component is limited to Ontario. That mixed sample offers useful examples, with its limits visible. A family should identify the province, intended grade and qualification before comparing the school’s current enrolment conditions.

Sources and selection method

60 Canadian CAIS records with secondary grades and 40 Ontario public secondary records; geographic round-robin.

Public-school coverage is Ontario only. Provincial qualifications differ; no performance rank.

Source: CAIS directory and Ontario public-school contact list. Source records checked 2026-10-01. Current admissions, courses and fees require the institution’s own current evidence. Each row links to its source.

Understand the provincial qualification

The subjects and credits needed to complete secondary education vary by province and student route. Ask the school or board how prior learning is assessed after a move. Grade numbering and completion timing can differ from the student’s previous system. For Ontario, use the current official diploma requirements and the school’s available course list. A directory record does not certify that a pupil will graduate by a particular date.

Separate public and private admission

Ontario public-school access can depend on address, board policy, programme and applicant status. A specialist programme may use a separate process. Private schools set their own published admission procedures. International-student tuition or residence requirements need a direct check. The table’s school type helps prevent a comparison that assumes every listed institution is available through the same route.

Check courses and progression

Older students need to compare the actual subjects required for the next stage. Ask about prerequisites, advanced courses, vocational options and how timetable conflicts are managed. A school’s general reputation cannot establish that a course will run in the intended year. University or college entry requires the receiving institution’s current rules. Save the relevant course and admission URLs in the comparison sheet.

Read outcomes and support carefully

An aggregate result should be tied to its cohort, year and definition. Schools with different admissions and pupil populations are not directly comparable using one headline. Ask how progress is monitored and how learning or language support is arranged. Guidance counselling, attendance procedures and help during a transfer matter to daily experience. A row number here carries no performance judgement.

Make enrolment practical

A family needs the actual journey, term dates and documents required for enrolment. Compare transport, meals, devices and extracurricular charges even where ordinary tuition is not charged. International and private-school offers need an itemized fee and refund schedule. Ask what happens if arrival is delayed or a course changes. Keep the school’s written confirmation rather than assuming that directory inclusion means a seat is available.

Decision checklist

  • Identify the provincial qualification
  • Confirm enrolment eligibility
  • Check required senior subjects
  • Map the daily commute
  • Save the course and fee evidence

Shortlist worksheet

For three feasible options, record the exact programme or year group, current eligibility, full cost, support provision, travel and the official evidence date. Leave a missing answer unresolved instead of estimating it.

Questions before you decide

Is this a national public-school ranking?

No. Public records in this sample come from Ontario only.

Which grades count as secondary here?

The source grade range or Secondary classification is used; verify the intended grade directly.

Do all schools follow Ontario’s diploma?

No. Province and school provision determine the qualification.

Does a public-school listing establish free access for every applicant?

No. Applicant status and board rules need checking.

Are the school numbers ranks?

No. They identify entries in a geographic sample.

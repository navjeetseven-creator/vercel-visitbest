100 US Public High Schools: A Guide Across All 50 States

Two regular public high schools per state. NCES 2023–24 version 1a directory. Row numbers are not ranks.

A national high-school list cannot replace a local enrollment decision. This guide uses the NCES Common Core of Data 2023–24 directory, version 1a, to sample two regular public high schools from each state. The selected records offer grades 9 and 12. Some also teach younger pupils. The complete earlier file provides consistent 50-state coverage; a newer preliminary download omitted two states. The table preserves identifiers, district and grade information. It does not identify the highest-performing schools.

Check the lawful enrollment route

Public-school access can depend on residence, district boundaries and published choice or transfer rules. Inclusion here creates no admission entitlement. A moving family needs the school assigned to the actual address. The NCES identifier helps distinguish similar names. Historical directory data needs a current district check for closures, reorganizations and programme changes. A neighborhood description or old map is not a complete enrollment decision.

Compare actual course access

The current catalogue can show mathematics, science, languages, arts and career education. A listed advanced class does not guarantee that it runs or that the pupil can enter. Prerequisites and schedules matter. Transferring students need a credit assessment. Language development, special education and academic counselling need a defined access process. Families can ask who helps when a pupil falls behind and how schedule changes are handled.

Read state reports with their definitions

Graduation, attainment and growth describe different outcomes. State tests and standards can differ. A national score assembled from incompatible measures can mislead. The year, cohort and participation rate matter. Families can read the improvement plan and ask how gaps are addressed. The directory is not an independent safety or teaching rating. An open school status supplies no such conclusion.

Plan a workable school day

Start times, transport and travel after activities affect participation. Families can compare clubs, sport and arts that the student can actually attend. A documented learning plan needs a transition discussion with the receiving district. Medical or accessibility needs require the actual arrangements. The final evidence sheet can record eligibility, courses, support contact, commute and unanswered questions. Local official registration information completes the decision.

Decision checklist

  • Verify attendance boundaries
  • Read the course catalogue
  • Ask about credit transfer
  • Plan support and transport
  • Row number is not a rank

Shortlist worksheet

For three feasible options, record the exact programme or year group, current eligibility, full cost, support provision, travel and the official evidence date. Leave a missing answer unresolved instead of estimating it.

Questions before you decide

Are these the highest-scoring schools?

No. This is a geographic directory sample.

Can every student apply everywhere?

No. District boundaries and enrollment rules matter.

Why can grades start before 9?

Combined schools can offer earlier grades while meeting the grades 9 and 12 filter.

Are private schools included?

No. This source is the public-school CCD directory.

Can state scores be compared directly?

Only with compatible definitions, cohorts, tests and years.

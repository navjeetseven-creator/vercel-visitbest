Top 100 International Schools Worldwide: A 15-Country Cambridge Guide

A 15-country Cambridge directory sample, not an exhaustive world ranking or an IB authorization list.

A worldwide school comparison needs a common source and clear limits. This guide samples 100 Cambridge-directory school records across 15 countries. Each selected name contains both “International” and “School” and has a listed website. The method supplies a checkable directory shortlist, rather than a claim that these are the world’s best schools. The table deliberately records country, source and directory information so a family can move from a broad search to the campus’s current admission details.

Read the source before reading the list

The Cambridge directory is used to verify a school record and a published website. The naming filter excludes some international schools and includes only one awarding body’s network. It therefore cannot measure the entire global market. A table number is not a rank. The country sample is Brazil, China, France, Germany, Indonesia, Japan, Kenya, Malaysia, Mexico, Singapore, South Africa, Switzerland, Thailand, the UAE and the UK.

Compare the award rather than the brand

A global school brand can operate several campuses with different subject combinations. Ask for the award, examination board, offered subjects and year-group structure at the actual campus. Check whether a move between branches requires a new application. A Cambridge listing does not establish ownership, boarding provision or IB authorization. Each additional claim needs the appropriate official evidence.

Plan the transfer between systems

A child’s age can map to a different year group after moving country. Ask how the receiving school assesses prior learning and what documents it needs. Keep transcripts, subject descriptions and support plans available. Older students should check whether a transition affects examination entries or qualification completion. The school’s current policy is more useful than an assurance that an international curriculum makes every move effortless.

Compare provision and affordability

Use one worksheet for the same year group across campuses. Record the full annual cost, deposit, transport, language support, learning support and withdrawal terms. Ask how activities and examinations are charged. Compare ordinary timetables and support capacity rather than promotional awards. Inspection or accreditation evidence should be linked to the correct campus and date. Keep missing information visibly unresolved.

Make a decision with current written evidence

Reduce the global table to destinations that fit the family’s work, residence and financial arrangements. Then confirm eligibility, places and the curriculum directly. Ask about the process for raising concerns and how parents receive progress information. Save current fee schedules and written offers with their validity dates. A directory shortlist is useful when it leads to a documented, feasible choice for the child.

Decision checklist

  • Read the sample limits
  • Match the child’s year group
  • Confirm campus qualifications
  • Compare included services
  • Keep the dated written offer

Shortlist worksheet

For three feasible options, record the exact programme or year group, current eligibility, full cost, support provision, travel and the official evidence date. Leave a missing answer unresolved instead of estimating it.

Questions before you decide

Does this cover every international school?

No. It is a 15-country sample from one directory using a stated name filter.

Does a listing confirm boarding?

No. Boarding requires a separate campus-level check.

Are fees directly comparable across countries?

Only after matching the year group, currency and included services.

Does Cambridge registration mean IB authorization?

No. They are separate systems and evidence sources.

Can the guide guarantee a transfer place?

No. Admission and available places are decided by the receiving school.

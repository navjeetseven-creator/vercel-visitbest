What this hub covers

This hub covers schools beyond a single national system through three guides, each a sample drawn from named directories rather than a VisitBest judgement of the world’s best schools. One guide samples 100 boarding schools across eight countries from the TABS public school directory and England’s official school records, with coverage concentrated in the USA and England. A second samples 100 international schools across 15 countries from the Cambridge International directory, selecting school names that contain both “International” and “School” and carry a listed website. The third sets 100 private schools side by side across three systems: 35 English independent schools, 35 Canadian CAIS-directory schools and 30 Victorian non-government schools in Australia. The hub does not cover every country, every school type or every curriculum, and a school’s absence from a table says nothing about its quality.

Directory sample or ranking? Read the label

None of the three tables in this hub is a ranking, and the number beside a school is a list position for navigation, not a measure of quality. The boarding guide is a directory sample: its 100 records combine 35 English independent boarding schools with TABS-directory records from the USA and the other sampled countries, so the order reflects the sampling and the geography, not examination results or the standard of residential care — membership or a directory boarding designation does not independently verify the care a school provides today. The international-schools guide is one awarding body’s directory filtered by a naming rule: it can only include Cambridge schools whose names contain both “International” and “School”, it excludes many international schools, and a Cambridge listing does not establish a school’s ownership, boarding provision or IB authorisation, which are separate systems and separate evidence. The private-schools guide combines three directories whose definitions of a private or independent school differ — English independent-school records, Canadian CAIS membership and Victorian school-location records — and membership, legal registration and academic performance are distinct evidence types that one shared table cannot merge into a single quality badge. In each table, read a row as a sourced record that a school exists and met the guide’s stated filter when the source records were checked — not as proof that it is better than the school above or below it, and not as confirmation of eligibility, a boarding place or a fee.

Before you apply: five checks

  1. Start with the award the child will actually leave with, checked at the intended campus: a global school brand can operate several campuses with different subject combinations, and a move between branches can require a new application, so ask for the award, examination board, offered subjects and year-group structure at the actual campus. A listing in the Cambridge directory does not establish IB authorisation, and qualification recognition depends on the receiving institution or regulator, not on a school’s presence in a guide.
  2. Confirm eligibility and the admissions route for the exact year group and intake: a directory records that a school exists, not that it has places or that a child qualifies. Age cut-offs and year-group names differ between countries, so a child can map to a different year group after a move; ask how the receiving school assesses prior learning, keep transcripts, subject descriptions and support plans available, and check whether the timing of a move affects an older student’s examination entries or qualification completion. Where boarding is part of the plan, confirm separately that it is offered at the intended campus and for the child’s age, and which arrangement applies — full, weekly or occasional — because day admission does not necessarily include a boarding place.
  3. Check the language of instruction and the support around the child: confirm the teaching language for the child’s actual classes and what language support is offered to a pupil arriving from another system, because an international curriculum does not by itself make every transfer effortless. Compare ordinary timetables, learning support and support capacity rather than promotional awards. For boarders, ask how new pupils are introduced, who responds to a problem overnight, how parents receive updates and progress information, and what counselling and medical arrangements are in place — matched to the child’s needs rather than to photographs of facilities.
  4. Take documents, dates and terms from official sources only: the guides are explicit that current admissions, courses and fees require the institution’s own current evidence, so use the school’s current publications for application dates, required documents and offer terms. Save the current fee schedule and any written offer with its validity date, read the deposit, withdrawal and refund terms before paying anything, link any inspection or accreditation claim to the correct campus and date, and keep a missing answer visibly unresolved on your shortlist instead of estimating it.
  5. Budget the complete cost, including boarding and the move itself: tuition plus deposits, uniforms, devices, activities, examination charges and transport for the same year group at each campus, compared in the original quoted currency with any conversion kept separate and dated. Where boarding is needed, add accommodation, meals, laundry, insurance and travel, and check who arranges airport transfers, holiday accommodation and guardianship, because weekend and holiday arrangements change both the routine and the bill. Residence, visa and guardian requirements depend on the destination, the child’s age and the school, and must be verified through the competent authority and the school’s current instructions — no table in this hub can settle them.

For the country and regional school comparisons, start from the School Comparison Guides Hub, which links the national and regional school hubs.

How to use the comparisons

  1. Read the source, edition and geographic limits.
  2. Shortlist feasible programmes or year groups.
  3. Save current admission, qualification and fee evidence.
  4. Resolve missing answers with the actual campus.

A directory listing, membership record and academic performance measure answer different questions. They are labelled separately throughout these guides.
